How does Couples & Marriage work?

💍 How Couples Therapy Works

Couples therapy is a safe space where two partners work with a therapist to improve their relationship, communication, and emotional connection. It’s not about “taking sides” — the therapist supports both partners equally.

🔑 What Happens in Sessions
1. Understanding the Relationship
• The therapist asks about the history of the relationship, patterns of conflict, and each partner’s perspective.
2. Identifying Negative Cycles
• Many couples get stuck in repeated patterns (criticism–defensiveness, pursue–withdraw, etc.). Therapy helps uncover these cycles.
3. Building Skills & Awareness
• Couples learn healthier ways to communicate, listen, and express needs.
4. Healing Deeper Wounds
• Emotional injuries, betrayals, or attachment fears are processed in a supportive setting.
5. Creating New Habits
• Partners practice new strategies between sessions to strengthen connection, trust, and intimacy.

⸻

🧠 Common Approaches Therapists Use
• Gottman Method: Research-based tools for communication, conflict resolution, and building friendship.
• Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT): Helps couples understand their emotional needs and build secure bonds.
• Integrative Approaches: Combining structure (like Gottman) with emotional depth (like EFT) for lasting change.

⸻

🌟 Benefits of Couples Therapy
• Better communication & conflict management
• Deeper intimacy and trust
• Greater understanding of each other’s needs
• Tools to prevent small issues from becoming bigger
• Renewed sense of teamwork and connection

✅ In short: Couples therapy works by breaking harmful cycles, teaching healthier ways to connect, and helping partners rebuild love, trust, and understanding.

The Ache That Doesn’t Kill You: On Heartbreak, Loss, and Learning to Live With Grief

There is a particular silence that follows loss.
Not the quiet of peace but the kind that roars in your ears when the world continues as if nothing has changed.

Someone laughs.
Traffic moves.
Notifications ping.
And you stand there thinking, How is everyone functioning when my world has collapsed?

Heartbreak and grief are not just emotions. They are experiences that reorganize you.
You are never the same after this loss.

Heartbreak Is Not Just Romantic

We often associate heartbreak with romantic endings. But heartbreak is broader than that.

It is:
• The death of a parent.
• The loss of a child.
• A friendship that shattered.
• A marriage that dissolved.
• A future you believed in that no longer exists.

When my father passed away, I realized something profound: grief is love with nowhere to go. It doesn’t disappear. It transforms.

The pain you feel is not weakness. It is evidence of attachment. Of meaning. Of having loved deeply.

The Body Grieves Too

Grief is not only psychological, it is biological.

You may notice:
• Exhaustion that sleep doesn’t fix
• Brain fog
• Tightness in your chest
• Changes in appetite
• Sudden waves of panic or sadness

As a psychologist, I often tell my clients: You are not “going crazy.” Your nervous system is overwhelmed.

When we lose someone we love, our brain experiences it as a threat to survival. The attachment bond that’s wired deeply into us has been severed. Your body responds accordingly with pain.

Grief is not a disorder.
It is a nervous system trying to understand absence.

The Myth of “Closure”
People say:
• “Be strong.”
• “Time heals.”
• “They’re in a better place.”

But grief does not respond to clichés.

There is no clean closure. There is integration.

You do not “move on” from someone you loved.
You move forward with their memory woven into your life differently.

Some days you will function well.
Some days a smell, a song, or a random Tuesday afternoon will undo you.

Know that you are not weak for hurting & not dramatic for grieving & not broken because you can’t let it go.

They live inside you.

What is CBT?
Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) is a highly effective, evidence-based therapeutic approach widely used to treat a variety of mental health conditions, including:
• Depression
• Anxiety disorders
• Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD)
• Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD)
• Phobias
• Eating disorders
• Insomnia
• Relationship issues

Key Principles of CBT:
1. Thoughts, Feelings, and Behaviors Are Interconnected
CBT helps clients identify and challenge negative or distorted thought patterns that influence emotions and actions.
2. Structured and Goal-Oriented
Sessions are focused on specific goals, with a clear agenda and practical techniques.
3. Skill-Building
Clients learn coping strategies such as thought records, relaxation techniques, and problem-solving skills to manage challenges effectively.
4. Present-Focused
While it acknowledges past experiences, CBT primarily focuses on current problems and finding solutions.

Techniques Used in CBT:
• Cognitive Restructuring: Identifying and reframing negative thinking patterns.
• Behavioral Activation: Encouraging positive activities to improve mood and energy.
• Exposure Therapy: Gradually facing fears in a controlled and safe way.
• Mindfulness and Relaxation: Reducing stress and promoting self-awareness.
• Homework Assignments: Practicing skills outside therapy sessions.

Dr. Mina Fares

Gaia’s Consultant Psychiatrist

Psychiatrists are medical doctors specialized in diagnosing, treating, and preventing mental illnesses and emotional disorders. They can help in the following ways:

1. **Diagnosis**: Psychiatrists can accurately diagnose mental health conditions through comprehensive evaluations, including physical exams, laboratory tests, and psychological assessments.
2. **Medication Management**: They can prescribe and manage medications to help balance brain chemistry and alleviate symptoms of mental health disorders.
3. **Therapy**: Some psychiatrists provide psychotherapy (talk therapy) to help patients understand their conditions and develop coping strategies.
4. **Crisis Intervention**: They can provide immediate help during mental health crises, such as severe depression, suicidal thoughts, or psychotic episodes.
5. **Collaborative Care**: Psychiatrists often work with other healthcare providers, including psychologists, social workers, and primary care doctors, to create a comprehensive treatment plan.
6. **Education and Support**: They educate patients and their families about mental health conditions, treatment options, and how to manage symptoms.
7. **Specialized Treatments**: For severe or treatment-resistant conditions, psychiatrists may provide or refer patients for specialized treatments like electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) or transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS).
